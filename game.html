The Game

Night Trail

A 2D top-down adventure in Godot 4. Cozy days and tense nights give the game its rhythm, and light is the thing that keeps you going.

Download the pitch (PDF)

Overview

A lone hiker sets out on a multi-day trek to a high mountain pass. Days are warm and cozy: explore autumn trails, gather supplies and meet other hikers at campsites. When the sun sets, the world falls into darkness, and the only safety is the light you carry.

Aim: make a 2D top-down adventure where the contrast between cozy days and tense nights creates its own emotional rhythm.

Objectives

  1. A light-as-resource system (headlamp, torches, campfires) using Godot 2D lighting
  2. A day/night cycle that changes visuals, audio and gameplay
  3. 3 hand-crafted trail sections: forest, ridge, pass
  4. A playable vertical slice of 20–30 minutes
  5. Playtests with at least 5 players, and changes based on their feedback

Main mechanic: light as a resource

Every day follows the same loop. You repeat it until you reach the pass.

Gatherwood, batteries, food
Sunsetreach camp in time?
Survive nightlight keeps you safe
Dawnprogress and reward

Repeat each day until you reach the pass

Day = preparation

Collect firewood, batteries and food. Plan the route to the next campsite before sunset.

Night = spending

The headlamp drains, torches burn out, campfires need fuel. Darkness hides cliffs and wildlife.

The core decision

Push on in the dark to gain ground, or make camp and burn your supplies to stay safe?

Soft failure: if you lose your light, you wake up at the last campsite with fewer items. You never lose your progress.

Aesthetic: day & night

Top-down pixel art with 16×16 / 32×32 tiles. The same forest feels cozy by day and tense by night. The lighting does the storytelling.

Visual assets

  • Top-down forest and mountain tilesets
  • Hiker sprite with a 4–8 direction walk cycle
  • Soft radial light textures for the lights
  • Edits in Aseprite / LibreSprite

Audio

  • Day: birdsong, wind, footsteps on leaves
  • Night: crickets, distant animals, crackling fire
  • Sounds from Freesound (CC0 / CC-BY), credited in the game

The AI images are only for the pitch and this blog. All assets in the final game will be properly licensed.

Story

The hiker returns to a mountain trail they once walked with someone close to them, to finish the route they never completed together. The story is told through notes left at campsites, talks with other hikers and the environment itself. It is light and optional: it rewards players who explore, but never blocks play.

Act 1

The Forest

Familiar autumn woods. Notes from an old trip hint at someone who walked here before.

Act 2

The Ridge

Exposed, colder, longer nights. Other hikers share pieces of the story around the fire.

Act 3

The Pass

The hardest night. Reaching the top completes the route they never finished together.

Technology

Engine: Godot 4. It is open source (MIT, no royalties), has built-in 2D lighting, and saves scenes as text files, which works well with version control.

View: 2D, 3/4 top-down. Not 3D. Objects are sorted by height on screen, so the player can walk behind trees.

Lighting
2D point lights, light occluders, a colour tint for the whole world
World
Tile maps, Y-sorting for depth
AI
Finite-state machines, navigation agents
Effects
GPU particles (fire, rain), fog shaders
Tools
GDScript, Git/GitHub, Aseprite, Claude Code

Methodology: Agile, Scrum of One

I work in six 2-week sprints. Each sprint goes: product backlog → sprint planning → 2-week sprint → playable build → review & playtest → retrospective. Feedback goes back into the backlog.

How I use it

  • The backlog is built from the MoSCoW list
  • Every sprint ends with a playable build
  • Short playtests tune how light and darkness feel, early on
  • A sprint board on GitHub Projects / Trello

Why not Waterfall

You can only judge game feel by playing. Requirements will change after each playtest, so a fixed plan up front would not hold.

Ethics, legal & PEGI

7PEGI

Expected rating: PEGI 7. Some mildly frightening scenes and sounds at night. No violence, gore, gambling or in-game purchases.

Copyright

Only assets with clear licences (CC0, CC-BY or purchased), with a full credits screen. AI images are used for pitch concepts only.

Ethical

No loot boxes or manipulative design. Soft failure. Accessibility: brightness setting, colour-blind-safe UI, key rebinding.

Social

Encourages interest in nature and responsible hiking ("leave no trace" notes). The game is offline, so there is no chat to moderate.

Data security

Local save files only: no accounts, analytics or personal data, so UK GDPR risk is minimal. Playtest feedback is anonymised.
